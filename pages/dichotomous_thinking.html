Dichotomous Thinking

What is Dichotomous Thinking?

Its thinking in a way of "all or nothing"

For example, if there is a task A. Lets assume it takes 2 hours to finish this task.

However, you realise that you don't have 2 hours of freetime to finish this task due to some reason. And can only afford 1 hour.

The most logical thing to do is to work on this task for an hour and finish 50% and maybe finish the other 50 tomorrow.

However in this way of thinking, you conclude that since you cannot finish the task today, it is better to not work on it at all.

And often in reality (atleast in my case), end up wasting that 1 hour of time anyway.

What causes this?

This is a purely psychological phenomenon. A false mindset that associates this thinking pattern with absolute truth. Therefore, it isn't that you know this thinking pattern is false and still do it, but instead it is convinced as absolute truth in your head.

Solution?

Now that we have established that it is merely a thinking pattern, the obvious solution would be to develop thinking patterns which are against this form. Which means, acting against this form of thinking. Which requires one to be aware of this problem in the first place. (This is a common theme I have observed across spirituality, that awareness is the ultimate solution, as being aware implies creating a separation between you and the other you're being aware of)

Inaccurate estimation

It is often also the case that sometimes a task seems "too hard" or even "impossible" in your head leading to procrastination. For example: Assume a task takes 5 hours but in your head you esimate it takes 24 hours which is not reasonable to be completed within a single day, therefore you conclude that it is better to not work on this at all

Delaying start until dependency resolution

I will start working on "X" only once scenario "Y" exists Realistic example: I will only start studying once I have my new notebook, pens, etc And this has happened to me with tamager, where I keep thinking that I will start working on my tasks once I have this task management system ready. But in reality, it is merely a tool which at best can improve my efficiency. BUT THERE IS NO EFFICIENCY if you don't work at all!!

Mutliple interests

This sort of mindset is also distruptive when you want to pursue multiple hobbies/interests. Becauase automatically unrealistic expectations arise from this all or nothing midndset And often it is the case that cutting down from a lot of interests is realistically the best way to manage multiple hobbies and not stacking more on top

The Unordered Section

So far, I've come to the realization that being aware of this mindset alone isn't sufficient. As multiple times during the day, I have observed
this mindset and it is far more often than I had initially anticipated and there was no change in behaviour even after this realization
*Therefore, this implies that this is a belief issue. That this mindset is getting manifested to the very core in me. And breaking it from there
is the first step. As naturally you literally cannot believe something that you know is to be false.
* This mindset therefore is composed of the following sub beliefs: * Perfectionism: that things need to be perfectly in order to attain something * That attaining the above perfectionism will solve all problems * I have also noticed that there is this implicit assumption that is forming when I think of doing the "best" course of action (i. e, following a realistic plan than a rigid unrealistic ideal plan) creates this pressure that you always have to do the best thing possible which consequentially removes any room for taking rest or taking time. * Also automatically there is this extrapolation of data. Where I cannot do anything casually without my mind thinking this will be "too hard"
in the long term, whereas in reality it could just be a weekly once thing instead of a daily thing

Practices

* Deliberately accepting imperfectionism and in that very act idenitfying that there is actually no such that as perfect or imperfect * Accepting the truth. * For example: let's start you want to start playing piano, ACCEPT that you cannot be the world's best piano player and that's totally okay! (and if you wanted to, you would have to give up everything else and dedicate yourself completely to playing piano) * This consequentially causes you to feel a sense of freedom and automatically live more in the present * Forcefully compute the negative consequences in following this mindset. * For example: assume you have to go to office and go to gym after that. But your bike is dirty. And you're too tired to clean it now, but due to this mindset, automatically this strict sequence is formed: * wash bike -> work in office -> workout in gym * But in reality you probably won't end up doing any of them! In which case it was much much better to go to office on your dirty bike and then hit gym, or at the very least hit the gym * So either start with the bare minimum (sort of a reverse pyramid thinking method) or accept the cost (nothing will be done today) and only then make a decision * Negatively reward perfectionism behaviour. Just recall how many times time has been wasted because of this mindset, no better way than to record this somewhere (logs) * Stop relying on any dependency to do something. "I will/can only do B once I have A". Flip it, "I will do B then I can have A if needed"
